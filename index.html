How Higher History is assessed and how to earn the marks. Based on the SQA course specification (May 2024) and recent SQA marking instructions.

The course at a glance

Paper 1 · 44 marks · 1 hr 30
British + European & World history. Two essays, 22 marks each: one from the British section, one from the European & World section. 40% of the course.
Paper 2 · 36 marks · 1 hr 30
Scottish history. One option, four questions: Evaluate the usefulness (8), How much do Sources… reveal (10), Explain (8), How fully (10). 33% of the course.
Assignment · 30 marks
An essay on a question you choose, written up in 1 hr 30 using a resource sheet (one side of A4, max 250 words). No resource sheet = 6-mark penalty. 27% of the course.
Essay question stems Paper 1
"To what extent…"
"How important…"
"Quote." How valid is this view?

The 22-mark essay (Paper 1)

Historical context · 3
Give at least two points of background, identify the key factors, and connect them to your line of argument.
Use of knowledge · 6
1 mark per developed point (extra detail, an example, evidence) that supports a factor and is relevant to the question.
Analysis · 6
Show relationships: links between factors, contradictions ("while… however…"), different groups or interpretations.
Evaluation · 4
Judge how important each factor was — ideally compared with others — building a line of argument. Use counter-arguments.
Conclusion · 3
A relative overall judgement between the factors, explaining why one mattered most. A summary only gets 1.
Structure that works
Intro (context + factors + line of argument) → 4–5 factor paragraphs (knowledge, analysis, judgement) → conclusion that ranks factors.

Paper 2 question types

Evaluate the usefulness of Source A… · 8
Provenance — author, type, purpose, timing — up to 4.
Content — quote/paraphrase and explain — up to 2.
Significant omission (your recall) — up to 3.
How fully does Source B explain… · 10
Start with a judgement ("partly explains…"). Interpreted source points up to 4. Recall (omission) up to 7. No judgement = max 2.
How much do Sources C and D reveal… · 10
State each source's overall viewpoint (1 each) and interpret its points (up to 2 each). Recall up to 6.
Explain the reasons… · 8
No source. 1 mark per accurate, relevant, explained reason. Give eight or more short, separate points.

Phrases that earn marks

Analysis
"This was linked to…" · "However, this was limited because…" · "While this helped the old, it did little for…"
Evaluation
"This was more important than X because…" · "This was the key factor, as without it…" · "On the other hand, historians such as… argue…"
Provenance
"Useful because it was written by a [who] in [when], at the height of [event], so…" — always link it to the question.
How fully judgement
"Source B partly explains the reasons for… It mentions… However, it does not mention…"

Exam tips

  • Answer the question asked. Link every paragraph back to the wording of the question.
  • Plan for 5 minutes. List 4–5 factors and decide your line of argument before you write.
  • Don't run out of time. About 45 minutes per essay. Incomplete papers are one of SQA's most common comments.
  • Judge as you go. Evaluation marks come from saying how important each factor was — and why.
  • In Paper 2, use recall. Points of significant omission carry most of the marks in 'How fully' answers.
  • Never leave a blank. A relevant, developed point can still earn a mark.
